# Actor models

The 3D view loads a mesh from the dump you point Ocarina Forge at. Converted glTF is cached on your machine, outside this repo. The sample dump has a few synthetic meshes so the path can be tested with no Nintendo files. A real dump is still required for real actors; the checklist is [testing-on-real-dump.md](testing-on-real-dump.md).

## Check scene meshes

Open **Problems → Audit meshes** to check the scene and its Static or Dynamic companion. The report groups placements by actor type. It distinguishes missing files, unsupported BFRES geometry, partial conversions, invalid glTF, and models waiting for conversion. Click a finding to focus a matching actor in the viewport.

The equivalent command is `python -m ocarina_forge mesh-audit <banc>`. Add `--model <gyaml>` to check one or more actor types, `--json` for the full report, or `--prepare-missing` to convert uncached models before checking. The audit checks the generated glTF and known converter omissions. Browser loading and visibility errors are reported separately in Problems.

## What gets drawn

For each actor name (`Gyaml`):

1. Open `Pack/Actor/<Gyaml>.pack.zs` (zstd dictionary 4) and read every `Component/ModelInfo/*.engine__component__ModelInfo.bgyml`. Each one, including entries under `SubModels`, has `ModelProjectName` and `FmdbName`.
2. Try `Model/<Project>.<Fmdb>.bfres.zs` (dictionary 1), then the uncompressed `.bfres`. When `FmdbName` is missing, the project name is used for both: `Model/<Project>.<Project>.bfres.zs`.
3. If that exact file is missing, try the other `Model/<Project>.*.bfres.zs` files, skipping `*.anim.bfres.zs`. When the exact file is on disk and its shapes are FSHP flag `0x10` (no decodable vertex buffer), the actor stays a box. Another fmdb in the same project is not drawn in its place. `Land_Kokiri_House_Outside_C_00` used to pick up `Land_Kokiri_House_Inside_C_03`.
4. Then `Model/<Gyaml>.<Gyaml>.bfres.zs`.
5. Older layouts still work: a `.bfres` stored inside the pack, a `Model/<name>.bfres` string in the pack, and `Model/<Gyaml>.bfres.zs`.

The first file that converts is the one shown. The same glTF is reused for every instance of that actor. Six downloads run at a time, nearest to the camera and in front of it first. Until one arrives, the actor is the labeled box, and the view stays usable. The progress chip counts models and says the rest are still loading. A static map is requested after the actors, and it is not part of picking, so a few hundred thousand triangles do not sit on the click path.

A scene draws the field from `TerrainArc`, not from the actor list. `Banc/GameBancParam/<Level>.game__banc__GameBancParam.bgyml` names `TerrainSceneName` and `TerrainScenePosition`. The mesh is `TerrainArc/<scene>.tscb`, and `<scene>_sub.tscb` when that file exists is a separate overlay. On Kokiri the overlay sits about 13–24 m above the actors, so it is not a finer copy of the walkable floor. Each scene keeps its own finest tile. A sub quad does not drop a main cell. Each tile’s `<name>.hght` and `<name>.mate` come from that scene’s own directory. `<name>.mate` is a block-linear 4-byte material grid and selects layers of `Tex/MaterialAlb.bntx.zs` (the same array `Mt_TerraMaterial_NNN` uses). Byte 0 and byte 1 are the two materials. 0x7f blends them halfway (a guess); an invalid index copies a neighbour instead of drawing magenta. The heightmap block at `+0x1C` is only a fallback, and a tile with neither is a flat earth colour. A coarse sample is dropped only where a finer tile of the same scene emitted a quad. An exact 0 left after that repair is a hole, not ground at the terrain origin. The odd rows of the 257th column are read from their packed GOB slot, so a dashed column of exact zeros is not a wall down to the origin. Remaining unwritten patches become holes instead of dropping the mesh to Y=0. The field is one atlas draw, with a skirt on each tile edge. It is front-side, and the skirts stay. **Sub terrain** starts off and shows the elevated layer as a wireframe reference. Snap uses the main ground. **Tile colors** paints the main tiles. Forge checks heightmap scenes for overlap with modeled landscape floors, paths, and roads. The check requires a cluster of buried surface samples with nearby ground contact before it fits a patch. The fit tapers into the original ground and keeps the separate sub layer out of the check. Opening a scene shows the available terrain first and prepares any fit in the background. The result is cached per map and age. The prepared Child Kokiri Forest ravine also restores height detail from the finest covering tiles and keeps its verified ladder and item clearances. Samples are about 2 m apart near the actors and coarser farther out. `<name>.<child|adult>.bake_ao.extm` can shade the ground. `<name>.custom.extm` is not decoded. The view uses triplanar UVs with an 8 m repeat, which is still a guess. The reason says `terrain heightmap`, which is not the proxy, so **Ground** starts on and the mesh is drawn. The TSCB bytes from `+0x0C` to `+0x5F` are not a hole mask, and GameBancParam only supplies the scene name, the origin, a parent, and a `_sub` name. `.mate` is a material index. A leftover exact 0 after the column repair is also a hole. When fewer than a fifth of the in-clip tiles have a `.hght` and at least 40 are missing, the reason says `sparse heightmap` and **Ground** starts off. The mesh is still there on the checkbox. A full field such as Kokiri stays on. It is not pickable, it does not cast shadows, and the actor LOD does not hide it. `python -m ocarina_forge ground-debug <banc> --at x,z prints the GameBancParam, the TSCB, each tile’s world box and raw height range, and the composed world height at that x,z (the finest height tile and any overlapping Land_* island, cliff, soil, or hill mesh, taking the highest surface). Actors named fairy or gossip are sampled the same way. The castle grass sheet is not clipped from this report until those heights are in hand. python -m ocarina_forge height-diff results.json --dump <dump> compares a Longshot world.ray height-check JSON against that same composed ground and against WaterPlane Front/Surface meshes. The preview fit still uses the narrower ground, floor, road, path, terrain, and rock-area cutouts (scene-terrain-v10, or v11 on Hyrule Field). Island, cliff, soil, and hill meshes are not baked into that visible mesh; viewport Snap queries `/api/ground` for the composed height instead.

If that TSCB is not in the dump, the view falls back to the first static-map bfres that exists:

- `Model/<Area>_NN.<Area>_NN.bfres.zs` (Kokiri Forest child is `Model/KokiriForest_01.KokiriForest_01.bfres.zs`)
- `Model/<Scene>.<Scene>.bfres.zs`
- the older single names `Model/<Scene>.bfres.zs`, `Model/<Area>.bfres.zs`, `Model/<Area>_Static.bfres.zs`
- the same stems under `Terrain/` and `Map/`

No map file and no TSCB means no extra object. It does not add a box. A TSCB that is present but yields no triangles does not fall back to the green stand-in.

Forge also restores fine height samples around entrances identified by a named transition actor and a nearby matching start position. This keeps narrow approaches such as the Water Temple entrance visible when the coarse ground mesh would bridge the depression. The refinement blends into the surrounding ground and stays in the map's fitted terrain cache.

At the Lake Hylia Zora building and Water Temple entrances, Forge also fits the ground beneath the verified floor faces. The fit follows the geometry under the doorway and along the approach, then tapers into the lakebed.

## What is decoded

Meshes and one base color. Vertices stay in bind pose. A shape with a skin count of zero is moved by its single bone. A skinned shape is already stored in model space, so it is drawn as stored. Animation is not read.

The base color is the albedo sampler. On a version-10 material that names its samplers, that is `_a0`, then `_a1` or `_a2` if `_a0` is missing. A normal, roughness, bake, or runtime slot (`_n0`, `_rgh0`, `bake0`, `tc`, `ta`, `MaterialCmb`) is not used as the color. Roughness, metal, and ambient occlusion are one extra image: R is occlusion, G is roughness, B is metal, and height is stored in alpha and not displaced. `_rgh0`, `_mt0`, and `_ao0` fill those channels. A packed `_rah` starts as roughness, AO, height and a packed `_rmh` starts as roughness, metal, height; the pixels override that when metal is bimodal, AO has a high mean, or height is the smoothest channel. A dedicated map wins over the packed channel. Layer 1 uses the same moss weight and the overlay UV, and `_n1` is the overlay normal when its sampler index is not `0xFF`. `_n2` and later layers stay unused. The factors are 1 when that image is present, so the texture is not multiplied back down to 0. `_em0` and `_ec0` are the glow. One map is the emissive texture. When one is gray and the other is colored, the gray map masks the color. When both carry color, `_em0` is the glow and the other is left out of the product. `_fx0` is an effect slot and `_th0` is thickness; neither is drawn as light. A material that also blends moss keeps the glow in the emissive slot and names the moss image on the blend extras. and the string pool is not a fallback once any sampler is named. The exception is `Mt_TerraMaterial_NNN`: it binds `ta=MaterialAlb` and `tc=MaterialCmb` with no `_a0`, and the colour is layer NNN of `Tex/MaterialAlb.bntx.zs`. That file is one ASTC 8×8 array (90 layers on the retail dump). Only the layer that material asks for is decoded, its alpha is forced opaque, the UVs repeat, and the PNG for that layer is cached. `Mt_Sand_Kokiri_Cliff` has no number. Version 10 does not store the version-9 render-info dictionary (`+0x18` and `+0x20` are the texture list, which is why those keys come back empty), so the sand cliff uses layer 16 and the log says that is a guess. A texture whose name is a normal, mask, blend, `_Fx`, `_Ind`, or `_Msk` map is not used on `_a0` even if the name also says Alb. `_a1` and `_a2` are still the overlay when the material has a `_c0` attribute, including a file named `Blend` such as `Land_HrlCastle_Wall_Brick_Blend_A_Alb`. A sampler index of `0xFF` is not blended. A used `_n1` and `_rah1` mix with the same `_c0` channel as `_a1`. `bake0`, `bake1`, and `_gn0` are not multiplied into the albedo. `CmnTex_BakeLightDefault` and `Dummy*` stay neutral placeholders. A glued suffix such as `BarkFx` counts too. A BC4 image is a single channel and is not a colour. If the sampler names are missing (the version-9 samples), a texture name that looks like an albedo (`_Alb`, `Albedo`, `Diffuse`) wins over `_Nrm`, and a file with no material texture can still pick one albedo-like name out of the string pool. Real textures are not inside the bfres. The viewer looks up `Tex/<TextureName>.bntx.zs` (dictionary 1), then the same stem with or without an `_Alb` suffix, then an image of that name inside a `.bntx` that holds several images. It does not substitute a different image from that file. Lookup is case-insensitive. An embedded BNTX is still used when the bfres has one.

`bake0` and `bake1` are lightmap slots, and this build does not sample them. The materials that name them bind `CmnTex_BakeLightDefault` or a `Dummy*` texture. That name is a shared placeholder, so the audit does not call it a missing file, and it is not a per-scene bake. The mesh carries a vertex attribute named `bake` beside `_u0` and `_c0`. That is the second UV a lightmap would use. The converter does not write it, and the viewer does not read it. A shape whose name is a bake shadow proxy is omitted on purpose. That mesh is not the lightmap. The heightmap’s occlusion is a different file, `TerrainArc/<scene>/<tile>.<Child|Adult>.bake_ao.extm`, and that grid is already applied to the ground. Because a castle wall’s `bake0` bind is the shared default, a unique per-scene lightmap is not the texture named on the material. This build does not search for another file and does not apply `bake0` or `bake1`.

`Model/KokiriForest_01.KokiriForest_01.bfres.zs` in the retail dump is a dummy: one material, no textures, positions only. It is used only when the scene has no TerrainArc TSCB. The converter writes that mesh as a flat-shaded earth-green material and the reason says `terrain proxy`. The view does not draw it. **Ground** is off for a proxy, and turning it on shows a neutral grid on y = 0, not the green triangles and not at the mesh’s median height. A loaded heightmap hides that grid; the mesh is the ground. A static map that does name an albedo, and a decoded heightmap, are shown with Ground on. The proxy is not looked up in `Tex/`. Path strips and moss mounds that already draw are actor models (`Land_*`, field props). They are not a substitute for the heightmap. Shapes omitted as InnerMesh, bake, fade, or water volume stay omitted; those names are not the field. FSHP flag `0x10` is still not decoded. `scripts/check_terrain.py` lists, for one scene, which heightmap tiles were drawn or skipped and which ground-like actors are still flag `0x10`.

Tile mode and block compression (BC1–BC7, ASTC, RGBA8) are decoded on the server to JPEG or PNG and packed into a glTF binary. An unlisted channel type on a known block family (the same codec as `0x1A01` with a different low byte, for example) uses that codec and the texture log names the id. The block height is the low 3 bits of BRTI `textureLayout` (`+0x34`). The mip-0 size does not replace that field. A 16-byte element is then placed in the Switch 2 GOB order; BC1 and BC4 stay on Tegra X1. [textures.md](textures.md) has the bit positions and the `tex-audit` scores. When several heights share that size and the stored one decodes as strong moiré, a much smoother height replaces it and the log says `swizzle log2 N, stored M looked like noise`. A non-square ASTC footprint whose other axis uses the same number of bytes is tried the same way, and the log says `footprint HxW, WxH looked like noise` when the swapped axis wins. 8×5 stays 8 wide and 5 tall unless that happens. A texture that stays noisy keeps the stored height and says so. ASTC ids `0x37`–`0x3A` are 10×8, 10×10, 12×10, and 12×12. A BC1 block with color0 ≤ color1 is the 3-colour mode. `0x1A01` and `0x1A06` are that codec (UNORM and sRGB), not a no-alpha variant. A block whose texels are all index 3 stays transparent, which is the sky on the FarView cards. A block that also uses another index is an opaque albedo: index 3 is the average of the two endpoints, not black. A 455×455 BC1 image is 114 blocks on a side, pitched to 120. Blocks 112 and 113 are the last GOB, and a zero block there repeats as the black border on the castle stone. The decoder copies the last full GOB block into that empty edge. Block-height log2 0 is a 115200-byte surface and log2 4 is 122880; a mip span that equals the smaller size stays at log2 0. `tex-debug` prints whether mip 0 matches that surface. An all-zero ASTC block on a last GOB row that is not a multiple of 8 is replaced with the block above. A failed decode, an unsupported format, or a solid magenta image is not bound: that part is a flat colour (the material’s colour parameter, or a neutral grey) and it does not reuse another part’s texture. Magenta texels in an otherwise real opaque image are filled from a neighbour, including a gutter up to 16 pixels and the opposite edge of a repeat. A cutout with a larger magenta region is punched instead. A triangle whose UVs jump across the texture is split so the seam does not sample a coarse mip of that gutter.

A material whose name, shader, or texture is water, a lake, a river, a stream, a moat, or the sea (not an NPC) is drawn unlit and translucent, with depth write off, after the opaque meshes. A usable albedo is kept and multiplied by the pale teal `(0.35, 0.55, 0.55, 0.2)`. `custom` on that material is blend. If it has no usable albedo, or the bound image is a normal map, a flat pale sheet, or still noisy after the swizzle retry, the mesh uses a generated ripple (`ocarina-forge-water`) tinted `(0.18, 0.42, 0.48, 0.62)` instead of the neutral cream colour. The reason says `water stand-in`. When a model has both `Mt_Water_Back_Flowmap` and `Mt_Water_Front_Flowmap`, only the front sheet is drawn, so the two coplanar layers do not stack into an opaque floor. **Water** in the viewport hides those sheets; it starts on. Shapes named as a water volume, a bake shadow proxy, fade-black, an inner mesh, an occluder, a navmesh, a minimap, a sight blocker, or a collision mesh are not drawn. An untextured box of at most 24 vertices that shares a model with a smaller UV’d mesh is dropped as a bounds box, which is the cream cube under the miniature courtyard. `Mt_Eye_Outer` and `Mt_Eyeshadow` are not drawn. An eyebrow does not reuse the face texture. Other blend materials also skip the depth write, so a translucent sheet does not stack into a green slab under the green fill light.

Version-10 alpha comes from the render-state string array. The u16 at FMAT `+0xA0` is 0 or 1 and is not that array’s length; the reader walks the 31 slots at the `+0x40` pointer and stops on a null or a non-string. Index 11 `mask` (or index 15 `true`) is glTF `MASK`, `translucent` is `BLEND`, and index 10 `both` is double-sided while `front` is single-sided. `custom` stays opaque except on a water material. Version-9 files still use the named `gsys_*` render info. The view turns on alpha-to-coverage for a mask. An opaque shape that shares its positions with a masked twin (FarView and FarView_Shaking) is dropped, so the opaque copy does not paint the punched sky black. `scripts/check_models.py` prints one block per material and says `textured` or `flat` for that material, then the shape names. A flat material also prints `flat fallback`. The summary counts shapes that received an image and shapes that used the flat colour. It does not call an actor textured because one of its materials was. The block still lists the sampler names, the texture each one references, which one was used as the albedo, the `Tex/` path, whether it decoded (format, size, swizzle, block height), the alpha mode, and the render-state word. A missing `Tex/` file borrows the nearest sibling albedo when one letter of the name differs and that file exists (`Rock_Kokiri_FieldProp_J_Alb` uses `FieldProp_I` or `FieldProp_K`). If there is no sibling, the flat colour follows the name: rock is grey-brown, vine or leaf is green, cloth or wood is tan, instead of the neutral cream. The log says which file was borrowed or which colour was used. A bake-only material stays flat. A water material with no usable albedo is the faint teal stand-in, not the cream flat colour. A mask whose alpha channel is empty keeps the cutout by using the colour as the alpha, so a beard card does not become a solid plane.

`python -m ocarina_forge material-debug <gyaml or bfres>` prints each material’s shader archive, shading model, attribute assign, sampler assign, option strings, and shader parameters. A dump-relative `Model/...bfres.zs` path is that file, including with `--strings`; it is not prefixed again. A retail version-10 assign at FMAT `+0x10` is a pointer block: a name pair, an attribute-name list, a byte index table (`0xFF` is unused), and a sampler-name list. `+0xA8` is printed the same way, including the block it points at. The byte table is the attribute index list and then the sampler index list. A sampler whose index is `0xFF` is unused, so the viewer does not blend it. The older dictionary assign is still read when `+0x10` is that record, including when the count bytes at `+0x44` do not match. When neither matches, the report prints each unread header qword (string, dictionary, or the first 24 bytes). A sampler line names the UV channel when the assign says `_u0`, `_u1`, or `_u2` for that sampler, the texture SRT whose name matches the sampler, and any option that talks about UVs or a projection (`triplanar`, `world`, `ManualUV`). A material name ending in `_ManualUV` is called out. The shapes section lists every vertex attribute (name, format, min and max), including `_c0` and `bake`, and still lists each UV set with its format, min and max, and the first few raw and decoded samples. The base colour stays `_u0`. An overlay uses the UV the assign names for that sampler, and `_u0` when the assign names none. A set that runs past 0..1 is the tiling set. `viewer albedo` is the sampler the viewer binds, with the Tex file’s pixel size when that file is in the dump. `python -m ocarina_forge tex-debug <texture> --png out.png` prints the BNTX header (format, width, height, depth, mips, tile mode, block height, image size, alignment) and writes the current decode. `--strings` prints the whole string pool. Names such as `gsys`, `texcoord`, `uv_`, `o_`, `enable_`, and shader-archive names are listed with the offsets that point at them. A material-name dictionary is not a shader-param table. A material that samples `_a0` and `_a1` (the castle slate roof, and the same shape on `Wall_Brick_Blend`, `Wall_Outside_A_Blend`, and `Floor_Stone` dirt blends) draws `_a1` over `_a0`. The mask is the `_c0` component a shader option names (`color0_g` and the same forms for r, b, and a). When no option names one, the only channel that varies by 0.02 or more is the mask. When red and green both vary, the mask is red: on `Mt_Land_HrlCastle_Roof_Slate_A` blue stays 0 and alpha stays 1, and green moves with red but is not the mix. The mix is a soft threshold from 0.35 to 0.65. The overlay keeps that sampler’s own texture scale and translate, so the moss tile is not the slate’s. **Blend mask** draws the weight instead of the two textures (white is the overlay). The terrain repeat stays 8 m. Selecting an actor shows the same rows under Materials. `python -m ocarina_forge material-audit [--json] [--out file]` walks every `Model/*.bfres` and `Model/*.bfres.zs`. The default print is one line per sampler-role signature (`albedo+normal+roughness+bake`), largest first, and a line `splits on attributes 4, render 2` when those materials do not share a vertex-attribute set, the exact sampler names, the render state, or the name tags. `--json` is the only JSON form: each group lists those splits with counts, plus a few example materials. The `unhandled` list, also sorted by count, is what the viewer still skips: an unknown sampler role, a sampler whose variation index is `0xFF`, an assign block that did not parse, and a texture with no `Tex/` file of that stem. A stem containing `Default` or `Dummy` (`CmnTex_BakeLightDefault`) is a placeholder, so it is not missing. The command reads headers only. Parsed headers are cached under the model cache in `material-audit/`, and the files are read in parallel. `--dump` is the Longshot folder when the editor has no dump configured. A sampler named as a normal (`_n0`) or an emission map is a second glTF texture when that image decodes. Roughness, metal, AO, and a used `_n1` are further images on that material. A mask slot is reported and does not recolour the albedo.

## Build-time actors

An After the Ocarina recipe that places an actor (the `stones:` list, or any other scene YAML row with a gyaml, a translate, and a banc) is drawn on that map before you build. It keeps the violet tint and the label “added by build, flag-gated” when the file names a flag. It is editable: the gizmo and the properties panel write `translate`, `rotate`, `scale`, and `params` back into that YAML (comments kept), and the build uses those values. Y in the file is the gizmo Y minus `y_offset`, so the offset is not applied twice. Hide and Delete ask, then remove the list entry. Duplicate adds a copy one metre along X. Undo, the Changes tab, and Revert use a snapshot taken the first time that file is edited (`.ocarina-forge/recipe-baseline.json`). Ctrl+Z applies the session shot in place: a transform updates the instance matrix, and the recipe file is written from that shot without reloading the scene. Saria’s file leaves `place: false` and does not add a ghost.

Areas, fog, triggers, sensors, collision, air walls, and `BGM_` regions are edge outlines even when the pack contains a cube. `FldObj_AirWall_…` is a system actor, not an object, so the System toggle and the Volumes chip hide it. The fill is drawn only for the volume that is selected, so a stack of areas cannot tint the forest. The fill does not write depth. A prop that is only a volume because its scale is 8 metres or more still draws its mesh. The outliner has a Volumes chip that hides every outline at once, plus a chip per category. The stage-bar Show / Lock / Hide for Areas and System is unchanged.

## Bind pose

Smooth skin (two to four weights) is already stored in model space and is left there. A shape with no per-vertex skin is stored in its bone’s local space and is moved by that bone’s world matrix, including when the rigid index is -1. Skin count 1 stores each vertex in a single bone’s space: `_i0` is a palette slot, not a colour, and the slot names a bone. Only a skin-count-1 bone that has a rigid-matrix index is applied, so a smooth bone is not applied twice. Bone flag `0x1000` is Euler XYZ in radians, composed as `Rz · Ry · Rx` (X first). Other bones are quaternions. Flag `0x800000` is segment-scale compensation. `Obj_HrlCastle_SwitchShutter_A_01` (Static #206) is the tilted door. Its panels are skin 0 on `DoubleDoor_L` and `DoubleDoor_R`, both rigid -1, with Euler `(±1.5708, 1.5708, 0)` and a translation of about ±2.65 m on X. That composition stands each panel upright, hinged to either side of the centre. `Land_HrlCastle_Gate_A_01` and `Land_HrlCastle_Cliff_A_04` stay where they are: their only bone is an identity root. The floating ivy is `Land_IvyClimbable_A_01` and `Land_IvyClimbable_A_02`. Those meshes are flat sheets authored along +Z, on an identity root, so the bake does not move them. Their actor Rotate is about `(1.5708, -1.29, 0)`. Banc Rotate uses the same `Rz · Ry · Rx`. three.js Euler `XYZ` is intrinsic and equals extrinsic ZYX, which leaves the sheet tipped. The view uses three.js order `ZYX`, so +Z hangs straight down and the sheet’s normal stays horizontal along the yaw. A yaw-only rotate, such as the gate, is unchanged. The gizmo, the properties fields, and snap write those same X, Y, Z components back. `python -m ocarina_forge model-debug --near x,y,z --radius 10 --banc Banc/HyruleCastle/HyruleCastle_Dynamic.bcett.byml.zs` lists nearby actors and the model file the viewer opens. Clicking a mesh selects it. Properties shows the name, the gyaml, and the hash, with one line for the model file and its triangle count. **Details** holds the material dump. The viewer does not read a Phive offset, a Dynamic open or closed state, a link parent, or a per-instance model override. The moat sheet is a water material (stream or moat in the name, or a water shader) whose albedo is a flow or normal map; it uses the ripple stand-in. The cream boxes at the far end of the courtyard, next to `BGM_Silence`, `FldObj_AirWall_CharacterSight_GenerateNavMesh_Body`, and the Hylia soldiers, are region actors and occluder or bounds shapes. The air wall and the silence region stay wireframes. A bounds cube that has a miniature of the courtyard on it is the untextured box in that model; the box is omitted and the detailed mesh keeps the bind pose. Kokiri and Kakariko actors are smooth-skinned or already in model space, so this bake does not move them.

The cache key is a hash of the source bytes, a version prefix (`models-57`), and a hash of `ocarina_forge/models.py`, `ocarina_forge/surface.py`, `formats/bfres.py`, `formats/meshlet.py`, `formats/bntx.py`, `formats/swizzle.py`, `formats/terrain.py`, `formats/gltf.py`, `formats/gpu.py`, `formats/meshopt.py`, and `frontend/tools/meshopt-worker.mjs`. Decoded BNTX images use `bntx-6`, so an old `bntx/*.bwtx` is not reused. Editing any of those misses old glbs even if the prefix was not bumped. Still bump the prefix when the glTF or texture output changes; the test `test_converter_output_is_pinned_to_the_cache_version` fails until the prefix and the pinned source stamp move together. Later work, not started, is listed in [ROADMAP.md](../ROADMAP.md). On startup the server deletes folders from older cache versions, and model files whose sidecar records a different prefix. Entries with no recorded version stay until the size cap evicts the least recently used files. The cap defaults to 20 GiB (`cacheLimit` or `OCARINA_FORGE_CACHE_LIMIT`, `0` for unlimited). See the README. A heightmap remembers both TSCBs, each `.hght`, `.mate`, and bake-ao file it read, the terrain folder mtimes, and `Tex/MaterialAlb.bntx.zs`. A second file, `src/<hash>.json`, remembers which texture files that glb was built from. The next open stats those files and returns the glb without parsing the bfres or decoding textures. The packed terrain atlas is a separate file under `atlas/`, keyed by the cell bytes and `atlas-1`, not by the glb prefix, so a converter bump reuses that image. Each unique BNTX is decoded once into `bntx/`, and the JPEG or PNG is reused from `img/` when another model shares those pixels. The view still batches meshes that share an albedo. Files live in `$OCARINA_FORGE_CACHE`, or `$XDG_CACHE_HOME/ocarina-forge/models`, or `~/.cache/ocarina-forge/models`. Cold converts run in four worker processes (`OCARINA_FORGE_WORKERS`, `0` or `1` keeps them in the server process). Those workers ignore Ctrl+C, and the pool shuts down without waiting when the server stops, so a terminal interrupt does not print a KeyboardInterrupt from each worker. A first scene open now waits for the server to prepare that scene and its Static/Dynamic sibling, then covers the viewport until browser download, decode, upload, and batching finish. The previous scene remains visible during server-side preparation, and the content-addressed cache makes later opens faster.

The editor does not draw that packed glb. It loads `model.glb?flat=1`, which is the mesh and a plain material, and, when Load textures is on, applies images from `GET /api/models/<Gyaml>/textures`. The manifest is cached as `texman/<digest>.json`. Each image is a file in the shared texture store. The lookup uses the original glTF node name. Three strips colons from the object name, so that sanitized name is not the key. The stage-bar Textures switch shows or hides those resident maps on the material the mesh already has. It does not fetch again and it does not drop the GPU copy. `describe_actor` without `flat` still packs images into a glb for the audit and older callers. See [formats.md](formats.md).

## Drawing

Every placeholder is an outline. A missing model, an FSHP flag `0x10` shape, a recipe that has not loaded, and a companion that has not loaded use an edge line plus a very faint fill, both with depth write off. A volume uses the outline only, and the faint fill appears while it is selected. A real mesh hides that box. Nothing in the placeholder path uses a solid material.

Static meshes that share an albedo (same image, alpha mode, and colour) go into one `BatchedMesh`. One multi-draw covers every copy and every other model on that material. The batch object stays at the origin. The batch id maps back to the actor for picking. Moving, selecting, or hiding an actor updates that instance’s matrix, colour, or visibility and leaves the other copies alone. Each instance is frustum-culled from its own world bounding sphere (the mesh sphere through that actor’s matrix). The distance is to the sphere surface, not to the world origin. **Draw** can add a metre cap (Auto keeps the size rules). Occlusion culling is not done. The camera far plane starts at 8000 m, frames to at least 4000 m or 80 times the scene radius, and grows as you fly. A prop with no far mesh under about 1.2 m is dropped past 56 m, under 3 m past 96 m, and under 8 m past 180 m. Those same distances, and a shape under about three pixels, switch a model that has a far mesh onto that coarse mesh instead of hiding it. The field is not distance-culled and is not frustum-culled. The status label shows the draw count. **Profile** shows fps, the instanced copies, how many of those are hidden, how many draws the same actors would cost without batching, and the triangle count. A still camera says `idle` instead of a frame rate. The measured baseline and each later slice are in [performance.md](performance.md). Shadows are off, and the pixel ratio is capped at 1.5.

A BFRES shape’s LOD list is an array of 0x38-byte mesh headers (`FSHP+0x5B` is the count). The glTF keeps LOD 0 and, when a later level has fewer triangles, that coarser level under a `lod1:` node. Shapes with no second level and more than about 300 triangles get a meshoptimizer far mesh (the border is locked; larger meshes keep a smaller fraction). That far mesh keeps UV seams: a triangle does not bridge a cut, and a tiled chart keeps its range. If Node or the `meshoptimizer` package is missing, that far mesh is the older grid quantize instead, and a position cell does not weld two vertices whose UVs differ. A unit-chart cut (u=0.98 beside u=0.02) is split so the mip does not pick up the gutter. A chart that tiles outside the unit square, such as a slate roof, is not folded: those coordinates are the ones the texture repeats with. The view draws one of them by screen size: the coarse mesh when the actor covers fewer than about 64 pixels. When the canvas size is not known yet, the coarse mesh starts past 44 m. Farther than the small-prop distances, the coarse mesh stays drawn. The selected actor stays on LOD 0. If the coarse mesh is missing, the full mesh stays. Submeshes of a single level are one mesh, not stacked LODs.

The camera is fly. Hovering the view focuses it, so WASD works without a click, unless a text field, a dropdown, or a dialog already has focus. Leaving the view blurs it again only when hover was what focused it. Right-button down hides the cursor (pointer lock) and turns with the mouse movement, including once the cursor would have left the view. Releasing the button, or Esc, gives the cursor back. WASD still moves while the button is held. Alt+left-drag orbits the point under the cursor, or the selection when the cursor misses, and middle-drag pans. There is no orbit/fly switch. The full binding list is the Shortcuts dialog and [controls.md](controls.md). The view renders when the camera moves, the selection or a transform changes, or a model arrives. Orbit damping finishes the frame it needs and then the loop stops. Labels are the selection, the actor under the cursor, and the nearest 24 when **All labels** is on. Picking uses a BVH (`three-mesh-bvh`) on the source mesh for the batch id the ray hit. A textured static map is merged per material and is not pickable. The untextured Kokiri stand-in is not in the scene unless Ground is on, and then it is the grid. Companion actors share the same batches; their color is dimmed, and **Pick companions** only changes whether the ray keeps the hit. Moving one still writes that actor’s own banc.

Albedo images are capped at 1024 on the long edge. An opaque image is stored as JPEG, a cutout stays PNG, and the GPU builds mipmaps. The geometry in the cached glb is `EXT_meshopt_compression`. Moss, the second ORM, and the second normal live in material extras. After packing, those images still exist and each one has a texture slot, so the loader is not asked for a texture that was dropped. Positions and texture coordinates stay float32, so a placed actor does not shift and a UV outside 0..1 is not squashed into the unit square. There is no `KHR_texture_transform` and no normalized texcoord. The Node helper is one process per Python process and speaks length-prefixed frames, so a large glb is not one JSON line. Its stderr is appended to `~/.cache/ocarina-forge/meshopt.log`. A helper that has exited has its pipes closed without a flush, and the next request starts a new one. A forked converter does not keep or kill the parent's helper. The view’s glTF loader has the meshopt decoder. KTX2 is not written: the Basis encoder that is available produces files three’s transcoder turns into an empty image, which would draw black. The KTX2 loader is connected at `/basis/` for a later encoder that round-trips. The API gzips glTF and JSON responses over 1 KB. One `POST /api/models/batch` returns every actor’s metadata. glb downloads then run eight at a time, nearest and in front of the camera first, and a converted glTF is reused from the browser cache (`immutable`, ETag is the digest) as well as the server cache.

A native shell (Tauri or Electron around this same three.js view, or a native engine) would not change the Kokiri cost. The time goes to draw submissions and to full-detail meshes in the distance, which is the same work on either side of a webview. The editor stays a browser page.

## Companion bancs

Opening a scene also loads its sibling: `_Static` with `_Dynamic`, same folder and the same age. The scene name in the bar gains “with Dynamic” or “with Static”. Those actors are drawn slightly dimmed, on by default. **Companion banc** hides them. **Pick companions** (on by default) lets you select them; off, they stay visible and clicks pass through. Selecting one switches the banc that Move, Rotate, and the properties panel write. The file you opened stays the host. Undo puts both diffs back.

Kokiri Forest child is the case this is for. The houses are in `KokiriForest_Child_Dynamic` (42 of 43). The NPCs are in Static, with their feet near y = 117, which is the floor of those houses. Static alone looks like the characters are floating.

`GET /api/models/<Gyaml>` returns `status` (`mesh` or `box`), `reason`, `source`, `triangles`, and `textures`. `GET /api/models/<Gyaml>/model.glb` is the file. Terrain uses `/api/terrain?banc=` and `/api/terrain/model.glb`.

## What stays a box

The properties panel shows the reason.

- No pack, and no `Model/<Project>.<Fmdb>.bfres.zs` or `Model/<Gyaml>.<Gyaml>.bfres.zs`. The properties panel lists the paths it tried.
- The pack is not a SARC, or the bfres is truncated or not a Switch `FRES` file.
- `.bfres.mc` (mesh codec). This viewer does not decode that stream.
- Packed meshlet shapes outside the supported layout stay boxes. Forge decodes rigid single-buffer BFRES 10.4 meshlets into the regular model cache and uses the model's authored near and far geometry. `model-debug` reports vertex and triangle counts or the decode error.
- The GPU buffer is an external section past the end of the file (the HasExternalGPU flag).
- A vertex format this reader does not know.
- The glTF request failed in the browser. The outline comes back and the reason is the load error. The warning strip shows that error.

## Picking

Areas, system actors (`StartPos`, `Player`, `Sound`, `AirWall`, `Ground`, names with `Anchor`, names ending in `Actor`), names containing `Trigger`, and land actors scaled to 8 metres or more on any axis are volumes. They are drawn as an edge outline. A fill is added only while that volume is selected. A ray prefers the closest solid (a real mesh, or an ordinary box). A volume is selected when the click is on its edge, or when the ray hits nothing else. Alt-click, or another click on the same stack, cycles. **Areas** and **System** can be shown, locked (visible, not pickable), or hidden. A real mesh on a volume actor is picked like any other mesh.
